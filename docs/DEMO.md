# Luma presentation walkthrough

Allow approximately 5–7 minutes. All interface labels and sample learning content are English.

## Before presenting

Start the local server, use one browser profile throughout, and open the app at normal zoom. The main screenshot viewport was 1440 × 900; long pages were captured in full. If you want the original starting state, choose **Reset demo data** and confirm before the presentation.

## 1. A classroom is the starting point

1. Select **Alex Morgan**, then **Continue with Google · Demo**.
2. Open **Software Engineering Fundamentals**.
3. Show the learning materials, then open **The Software Development Lifecycle**.

Suggested explanation: “Students enter a shared classroom, find the same instructor-provided material, and study without switching between disconnected tools.”

## 2. Turn a question into understanding

1. Click **How do Waterfall and Agile differ?** in the study-partner panel.
2. Show the prepared explanation and its **Source** link. The AI panel scrolls independently of the document.
3. Open **Explore animation**. Demonstrate **Next step**, **Play animation**, **Pause**, and **Reset animation**.

Suggested explanation: “The prototype demonstrates document-aware assistance and a real interactive teaching animation. AI responses and animation generation are prepared simulations in this iteration.”

## 3. Make the explanation yours

1. Close the animation and choose **Save to Wiki**.
2. Switch to **Write** and add a personal example.
3. Add `[[Requirements Analysis]]` or another existing note title. Use **Preview** to follow the link.
4. Open **Knowledge graph** to show the actual links among your notes.
5. Return to a note and use **Find a connection** to review a prepared AI suggestion. Nothing changes until you choose **Add to my note**.

Suggested explanation: “A useful answer becomes a note that students can edit and connect to what they already know.”

## 4. Share understanding

1. Choose **Publish to Market**, then **View publication**.
2. Switch to **Jamie Chen** using the top-right avatar.
3. Open **Wiki Market**, find **Waterfall vs Agile**, and choose **Copy to My Wiki**.
4. Show author attribution. Jamie now owns an independent editable copy.

To demonstrate classroom joining, choose **Join a class** as Jamie and enter **SE2026**. After a reset Jamie initially belongs only to Building with AI.

Suggested explanation: “Students can contribute useful explanations to a free community library. Publishing creates a snapshot; private edits do not silently change the shared version.”

## 5. Instructor and administrator perspectives

1. Switch to **Dr. Sarah Wilson** and open **Class management**.
2. Choose **Manage class**, then **Upload material**. Use a small Markdown/text file or PDF.
3. Open **Members** to show the roster and invitation code.
4. Optionally switch to **Taylor Brooks** and open **People & roles**.

For PDFs, a reload intentionally asks for reselection; binary bytes are kept only for the current session. Students' personal Wiki notes are not visible in another identity's Wiki.

## What to say about scope

This iteration validates the product flow and UI. Real Google OAuth, document extraction/retrieval, actual LLM integration, server-side permissions, shared storage and multi-user synchronization are future implementation work. The broader course assignment also includes activities beyond this prototype.
